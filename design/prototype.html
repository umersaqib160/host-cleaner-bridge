Design notes · English only, not part of the product
Open question for the client — property access

This screen assumes any cleaner on the list can get in. If entry depends on a physical key held by one person, the fallback order is fiction — we would need a "who holds the key" field and the cascade would have to respect it. Worth asking before this is built.

Open question — when the clean actually happens

Jobs are triggered by checkout here. If the client cleans the day before the next arrival instead, the trigger changes and "Next guest" becomes the scheduling anchor, not the checkout time.

Design notes · English only, not part of the product
Availability is not modelled yet — and it should be

Right now the cascade will offer a job to someone who is never free on Sundays, wait for the timeout, then move on — burning a message from the plan and 15 minutes for nothing. Recurring days off plus specific blackout dates would let the engine skip them silently. Ask the client how they currently know who is free.

Design notes · English only, not part of the product
This screen is the alternative to building an app

Everything a cleaner needs — job details, access code, accept or decline — arrives through a link in the text they already receive. No install, no store review, no adoption problem. If the client says their cleaners would not tap a link from an unknown number, this whole approach needs rethinking, which is why it is on the discovery list.

Language follows the person, not the property

Switch the tabs above: the same job renders in whichever language that cleaner was assigned. A Belgian owner can run the dashboard in Dutch or French while their Mexico City team reads Spanish and one English-speaking cleaner reads English — all from one template set.

The access code is doing a lot of work here

A lockbox code makes reassignment trivial — any cleaner in the fallback order can be sent. A physical key handed between people would break it. The prototype assumes a lockbox; confirm with the client.
